Why This Briefing Exists

"Wae Rebo is not a tourist attraction.
It is a living home."

Wae Rebo is a traditional Manggarai village nestled at 1,200 metres in the mountains of Flores. Its seven iconic Mbaru Niang — towering conical homes — have stood for over a thousand years and carry the cultural memory of the entire community.

The Lembaga Pengelola Budaya Wae Rebo (LPBW) is the village's official cultural governance body. They have published formal guidelines for all visitors — not as restrictions, but as the minimum standard of respect the community expects from anyone entering their ancestral home.

At EastXperience, we are fully aligned with these guidelines. This briefing is mandatory before your visit — not just because the rules say so, but because you deserve to arrive informed, respectful, and ready for something genuinely extraordinary.

Official LPBW Guidelines

Four Requirements.
Zero Exceptions.

Issued formally by the Lembaga Pengelola Budaya Wae Rebo to all tour operators, guides, and visitors. These apply to every guest on every visit.

Requirement 01
👗

Dress With Respect

All guests must wear clothing that reflects Manggarai adat values. The village community expects visitors to cover their shoulders, midriff, and legs to at least the knee at all times within the visit area.

Shirt with sleeves · No open back or stomach · Shorts/pants must reach the knee
Requirement 02
⏰

Begin Trek Before 16:00

The trek to Wae Rebo must begin no later than 16:00. Guests who arrive at the Information Office after 16:00 will be required to stay overnight at the base village and continue the trek the following morning.

Target start: 12:30–14:00 · Absolute limit: 16:00 WIT
Requirement 03
📋

Register at Kantor Informasi

Every guest must stop at the Wae Rebo Information Office before the trek begins. This is where registration, official entrance fees, and a formal cultural briefing from village representatives all take place.

No trekking before completing registration and briefing at the office
Requirement 04
🚫

No Alcohol or Narcotics

Alcohol and narcotics are strictly and completely prohibited throughout the entire Wae Rebo visit — from the Information Office to the village and back. Guides must also deliver a cultural briefing before any guest enters the village.

Strictly prohibited by village adat law · No exceptions whatsoever

Village Etiquette

How to Move Through
a Living Heritage Village

Wae Rebo is not a museum or a set piece. Families live, raise children, pray, and work inside these conical homes. Move through it with that awareness.

Always ask before photographing community members — especially elders and children. During ceremonies or rituals, put your camera away unless explicitly invited to photograph. Photography inside the Mbaru Niang is generally not permitted without the family's direct permission. If in doubt, don't shoot. The memory in your mind outlasts any photo.
If a ceremony or ritual is underway, follow your guide's instructions precisely. Do not enter ritual spaces, do not raise your voice, and do not touch or move any sacred objects or offering items. Some ceremonies are entirely closed to outside observation — accept this without question. You are a guest in their spiritual life, not a spectator in a performance.
Speak in low voices throughout the village, particularly in the mornings and evenings. No Bluetooth speakers, no loud music, no boisterous behaviour after dark. The silence of the highland is itself part of the Wae Rebo experience — one of the most precious things you will bring home. Protect it for the guests who come after you.
Follow your guide when moving around the village — do not wander independently. Do not enter any Mbaru Niang without being invited by the family inside. Do not approach altars, ritual areas, or communal sacred spaces. The central open area is shared, but private sections of each home are private in every sense of the word.
If you want to give back to the community, the most meaningful way is to purchase handwoven textiles (tenun) or locally grown coffee directly from artisans in the village. Direct cash gifts to individuals — especially children — can create imbalance and dependency. Discuss with your EastXperience guide if you'd like to support the community in a meaningful, respectful way.
There is no waste collection system at Wae Rebo. Everything you bring up, you carry back down. Pack your wrappers, bottles, tissues, and personal waste out with you. Minimise single-use plastics before you even start packing. If you see litter on the trail or in the village, pick it up — your example sets the standard for every guest who comes after you.

If Things Run Late

The 16:00 Rule

We always aim to arrive at the Kantor Informasi by midday. But mountains and roads don't always cooperate. Here's the protocol.

LPBW requires that all guests begin the trek before 16:00. If your group arrives at the Information Office after 16:00, the trek is postponed to the following morning. Your EastXperience guide will immediately arrange overnight accommodation in Denge village — and the trek begins at first light the next day.

✓ Arrive by 15:30

Registration → briefing → begin trek → arrive at Wae Rebo before dark. Your 2D1N program runs exactly as planned.

⚑ Arrive after 16:00

Trek postponed to dawn. Guide arranges homestay at Denge base village. Trek begins at first light. Extended program applies — your guide will coordinate everything.

This is the reason we depart Labuan Bajo at 07:00 sharp. Early pickup gives ample time for the road journey, lunch, gear preparation, and the Kantor Informasi stop — with a safe buffer before the 16:00 limit. Punctuality at pickup protects everyone's program.
